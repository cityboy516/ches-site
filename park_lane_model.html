Fixed assumptions and caveats

  • £400,000 purchase, 2% sourcing fee, £6,000 legals and contingency, SDLT £9,500 at non-residential rates (6+ dwellings in one transaction). Confirm that treatment with the conveyancer: it is worth about £20,500 against residential plus surcharge.
  • 70% LTV interest-only on the £400k, 3% product fee plus £1,400 valuation and application fees, all paid in cash rather than added to the loan.
  • 8 flats at £475 pcm now, rising to £600 pcm after refurb. Rents held flat in nominal terms. Refurb rolled out evenly over 3 years: 2 months void, then one month's rent as a letting fee, then 12% management on all rent. Tenancies average 2 years, with 1 month void and 1 month letting fee at each turnover.
  • Refurb capex is capitalised in full: excluded from the return numerator, included in the cash invested denominator and added to property value when spent, then growing at 3% pa. At the entry yield the rent uplift alone justifies about 88p in the £, so the last 12% relies on the block re-rating once fully refurbished.
  • No corporation tax. In an SPV interest is fully deductible, and much of a £15k flat refurb is revenue expenditure rather than capital, which cuts against capitalising all of it.
  • The IRR assumes a notional sale at the end of year 5, before sale costs and CGT.
  • Refurb timing is assumed, not known. If vacancies bunch, the year 1 to 3 cash drain is materially worse.
