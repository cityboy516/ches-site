Parental mortality in religious Jewish families

Monte Carlo model of how often children lose one or both parents before a given age, and how often parents die leaving young children. Families are simulated from the birth of the first child, with ONS England & Wales mortality by calendar year.

Method and basis

  • Mortality: ONS 2024-based past and projected period mortality rates (qx), England and Wales, principal projection, by single year of age and calendar year. Rates for 2000 to 2024 are actual ONS rates (including the COVID years); 2025 to 2074 are the ONS projection. After 2074, each age's average improvement over 2064 to 2074 is continued. Ages above 100 use the age 100 rate.
  • The mortality percentages scale the ONS rates; the extra improvement adjusts projected rates by (1 − i)year − 2024, so a positive value means lighter future mortality than ONS.
  • Family formation: the target number of children is drawn from a negative binomial with the given mean and SD (Poisson if the SD is at or below √mean), then held within the minimum and maximum. The mother's age at first birth is a truncated normal; the father's age is the mother's age plus a normal age gap, fixed for the family. Further births follow gamma-distributed intervals.
  • Births stop when the target is reached, when the next birth would put either parent outside the age limits, or when either parent has died. Realised family sizes are therefore a little below the target; the summary above shows the realised figures.
  • Both parents are alive at the first birth by construction. The two parents' deaths are independent. No divorce, remarriage, step-parents, twins or posthumous births. Children's own mortality is ignored, which slightly overstates children left behind at older ages.
  • Deaths are simulated in annual steps from the first birth, using the rate for the parent's age and the calendar year at the start of each step, with the date of death uniform within the year.
